# NIST's 2026 AI Risk Profile for Critical Infrastructure

On April 7, 2026, NIST announced a concept note for an AI Risk Management Framework profile on trustworthy AI in critical infrastructure. The proposed profile is intended to help operators consider risk-management practices when using AI-enabled capabilities.

## Why a sector profile matters

AI risk depends on how and where a system is used. A recommendation tool and a system involved in essential infrastructure have different failure consequences, operational constraints, and recovery needs. A sector-specific profile can help translate broad principles into questions that operators and suppliers can discuss.

## What the announcement does—and does not—say

The NIST page describes a concept note, not a completed profile, certification, or binding regulation. Organizations should not infer that a system is safe merely because a framework is referenced. They still need to understand the system's role, validate it in context, and prepare for failures.

## Practical steps for operators

Inventory where AI is used, identify dependencies and failure paths, and define who can stop or roll back a system. Evaluate performance under realistic conditions, monitor incidents and changes, and ensure people know how to escalate problems. Keep supplier claims separate from independently verified results.

The next step for interested teams is to read the NIST source and track its updates. Apply current sector obligations in parallel; voluntary guidance does not replace them.
