# AI Industry Briefing: What to Verify Before Adopting a Model

The AI landscape changes quickly, but product announcements are not the same as independent evidence. A careful reader asks what was measured, on which data, under what conditions, and whether the result applies to their own use case.

## Read benchmark claims in context

Check whether a reported result uses a public benchmark, a private evaluation, or a demonstration selected by the developer. Look for comparison baselines, sample sizes, task definitions, and failure rates. A score can improve while latency, inference cost, or performance on a specific user group remains poor.

## Separate capability from readiness

A model may complete a task in a controlled test but still require safeguards in production. Review data handling, tool permissions, logging, human review, and escalation paths. For consequential applications, require validation on representative data and a plan for monitoring after launch.

## Track the evidence, not just the headline

Maintain a short decision record with the model version, evidence reviewed, known limitations, and owner responsible for monitoring. Revisit the decision when the model, provider terms, data, or deployment context changes.

The Stanford AI Index is a useful source for broad annual indicators, while NIST provides risk-management guidance. Neither replaces task-specific testing. The most useful news is the kind that changes a measurable decision, not just a demo.
